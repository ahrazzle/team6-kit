# Website Copy for B2B / Operator Firms

## When to use
- User asks for website copy, a copy pack, landing-page text, or brand voice for a B2B / consulting / infra / AI firm.
- User has an agentic dev team and needs paste-ready copy, not prose or a strategy essay.
- User shares service pillars, past ventures, or a positioning angle and wants it turned into site copy.

## Core principles
1. **Clear beats clever.** Hero copy must pass a 5-second test — a smart 14-year-old gets it on first read.
2. **Client-first, not "what we did."** State the outcome the client gets, not your activity list. "We build data centres that stay up" > "Hands-on experience from feasibility through commissioning."
3. **Active voice, present tense for operations.** "We build" not "Built by us." "We ran" implies you exited — use "We built and ran."
4. **One idea per sentence.** Under ~20 words.
5. **No jargon without a 5-word explanation** on first use. ("Commissioning" = testing a site until it is live and approved.)
6. **End every section with an outcome the client can picture.**

## Workflow
1. Get the firm's actual scope (pillars/services) and at least one real proof point per pillar. Do not invent metrics — use `[ Metric ]` placeholders and ask.
2. Pick a positioning frame: **general contractor / integrated system / operator-at-risk**. Do NOT position a wide-scope firm as a narrow specialist — that contradicts the value.
3. Draft: hero (clear) → thesis (why integrated) → pillar overview → pillar detail pages → proof → comparison vs point firms → process → CTA.
4. **SELF-AUDIT against the copy rules before delivering.** The pack's own rules are the checklist. A real session shipped copy that violated its own "no jargon without explanation" rule (a section used "AI boutique" / "infra advisor" undefined). Catch that class of miss.
5. Compile as a structured pack: verbatim copy, section headers marked H1/H2/P, explicit dev instructions ("use verbatim, do not rewrite"), and a COPY RULES block at the end.

## Pitfalls (learned the hard way)
- **NEVER suggest adversarial or internal-only phrasing for client-facing copy.** "One throat to choke" reads as a threat to a founder or board. Use positive accountability: "One team. One call." or "Single point of accountability."
- **Do NOT put clever insider lines on the hero.** Keep them as a *manifesto pull-quote* option for visitors who already get the brand. Cold visitors will not decode "The AI boutique doesn't commission your facility" — it costs them too much.
- **Past-tense proof implies exit/failure.** "Ran mining as an operating business" suggests you shut it down. Use present-perfect or "built and ran."
- **A wide-scope firm's biggest trust risk is "does anyone do all this well?"** Kill that fear with process + proof, not with a line that reminds the visitor of specialists.
- **"White glove" and "end-to-end" are internal jargon** — avoid on the site, or prove the claim instead of stating it.
- **Flat, dry lists build trust only WITH hard proof beside them.** Operator voice ("We did X at our own risk") beats consultant voice ("We have experience in X").
